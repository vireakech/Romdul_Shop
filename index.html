About the product

Made from a single plank, and made to be played

Every Romdul instrument starts as seasoned hardwood — khnor, neang nuon or rosewood — that our artisans carve, assemble and polish over weeks rather than hours. Nothing is veneered, and nothing is rushed.

The voice is what matters. A Roneat rings with cool bronze, a Sralai carries over the gongs, and a Skor Thom speaks with a dry, wooden snap. Each piece is finished with natural lacquer and traditional Khmer motifs, so it looks the way it sounds.

  • Seasoned hardwood and natural materials — no veneer, no shortcuts
  • Tuned by working musicians before the piece leaves the workshop
  • Padded, crated and insured for tracked shipping worldwide

The sound of Khmer culture

ពិណពាទ្យ

Pinpeat

The classical court ensemble of gongs, xylophones, oboes and drums that accompanies royal ceremonies, classical dance and masked theatre.

មហោរី

Mohori

A gentler string-and-voice ensemble for weddings and celebrations, built around the Tro, the Khim and the long-necked Chapei.

អង្គរ

Angkorian roots

Instruments we still build today — the arched Pin, the barrel drums — are carved onto the walls of Angkor. We keep those ancient forms alive.

សិប្បកម្ម

Living craft

Each artisan in the workshop trained inside a family tradition, passing carving and tuning techniques from one generation to the next.
